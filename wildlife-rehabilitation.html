Wildlife rehabilitation

Care is a process, not a photo opportunity.

The project proposal focuses on the systems behind rehabilitation: safe intake, diagnostics, treatment, species-appropriate housing, recovery, translocation and release.

What is the project trying to improve?

CuteFela’s project material identifies capacity, equipment, enclosures, plastic waste, diagnostics, staffing and release planning as practical constraints that can limit the quality of wildlife rehabilitation.

From rescue to release

Every stage creates a different welfare problem to solve.

A rehabilitation centre is only as strong as the hand-offs between admission, clinical care, recovery and release. The proposal maps those needs across both facilities and operating protocols.

01Admit

Quiet intake, triage and records.

02Treat

Diagnostics, surgery and veterinary care.

03Recover

Species-appropriate enclosures and low-stress rehabilitation.

04Release

Translocation, suitable habitat and follow-up where possible.

What the proposal flags

Infrastructure matters because welfare depends on it.

Capacity: increasing wildlife admissions can overwhelm limited treatment and holding space.

Equipment: diagnostic, surgical and transport capacity needs to match the range of species being treated.

Housing: birds, reptiles, primates, deer, crocodilians and small cats need different recovery environments.

Release: rehabilitation only works when there are suitable places and protocols for animals to return to the wild.

From the project proposal

A primate nursery planned around care stages.

The proposal includes concept drawings for a primate nursery with spaces for new arrivals, infants, juveniles, enclosures, staff functions and controlled movement through the facility.

Concept material is shown for project context and should not be treated as a final construction drawing.

What good rehabilitation protects

Animal welfare

Less stress, safer treatment and recovery environments designed around the biology of the species.

Wild populations

Release decisions that do not create new disease, conflict or ecological risks.

Public trust

Clear protocols and transparent priorities make it easier for people to support rescue and rehabilitation responsibly.
